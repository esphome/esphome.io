The `usb_audio` speaker platform plays audio on a USB audio device, such as a USB sound card, DAC or speakerphone,
connected to the [USB Audio](/components/usb_audio/) component.

This platform only works on ESP32 variants with a USB OTG peripheral.

> [!WARNING]
> Audio and voice components consume a significant amount of resources (RAM, CPU) on the device.
>
> Crashes are likely to occur if you include too many in the same configuration. In particular, Bluetooth/BLE
> components are known to cause issues when used in combination with audio and voice components.

```yaml
# Example configuration entry
usb_audio:

speaker:
  - platform: usb_audio
    id: usb_speaker
```

## Configuration variables

- **sample_rate** (*Optional*, positive integer): The sample rate of the audio stream, in Hz. Must be between `8000`
  and `96000` and supported by the USB device. Defaults to `48000`.
- **bits_per_sample** (*Optional*, enum): The bit depth of the audio samples. One of `8bit`, `16bit`, `24bit` or
  `32bit`. Must be supported by the USB device. Defaults to `16bit`.
- **num_channels** (*Optional*, positive integer): The number of channels, `1` (mono) or `2` (stereo). Must be
  supported by the USB device. Defaults to `2`.
- **buffer_size** (*Optional*, positive integer): The size of the buffer between ESPHome and the USB driver, in
  bytes. Must hold at least two USB transfers (6 ms of audio, e.g. `1152` bytes at 48000 Hz, 16 bit, stereo).
  Defaults to `6400`, or to the minimum when that is larger.
- **write_timeout** (*Optional*, [Time](/guides/configuration-types/#time)): The maximum time a write waits for
  room in the buffer. Defaults to `20ms`.
- **usb_audio_id** (*Optional*, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The [USB Audio](/components/usb_audio/)
  component to use. Defaults to the only one in YAML.
- All other options from [Speaker Component](/components/speaker/).

The volume and mute state are passed to the USB device. If the device has no volume or mute control, a warning is
logged and the setting has no effect.

The ESP32 keeps a few milliseconds of audio queued on the USB bus, and the queue is refilled by a task. Writing to
flash stalls that task, so a flash write while audio plays leaves a short gap: for example the boot counter that
the [Safe Mode](/components/safe_mode/) component writes about a minute after each boot, saved preferences, or an OTA
update.

The stream takes about a second to start on the USB device. Until it runs, `play()` accepts no audio: it waits up
to `ticks_to_wait` and returns `0`. A lambda runs on the main loop, so call `play()` there with a wait of `0`
(or the two-argument form, which never blocks) and send the unsent audio again on a later loop; a blocking wait
stalls the loop and can trip the task watchdog.

## Media player

To stream media or play announcements on a USB speaker, use it with the
[Speaker Media Player](/components/media_player/speaker/). Use a [Mixer Speaker](/components/speaker/mixer/) to give
the announcement and media pipelines their own source speakers, and match their sample rate to the USB speaker, or
add a [Resampler Speaker](/components/speaker/resampler/).

```yaml
speaker:
  - platform: usb_audio
    id: usb_speaker
    sample_rate: 48000
    num_channels: 2
  - platform: mixer
    output_speaker: usb_speaker
    num_channels: 2
    source_speakers:
      - id: announcement_speaker
      - id: media_speaker

media_player:
  - platform: speaker
    name: USB Media Player
    announcement_pipeline:
      speaker: announcement_speaker
      format: FLAC
      sample_rate: 48000
      num_channels: 2
    media_pipeline:
      speaker: media_speaker
      format: FLAC
      sample_rate: 48000
      num_channels: 2
```
