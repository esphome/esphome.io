The `equitherm` climate platform is a weather compensated heating controller: it sets the boiler's flow temperature
from the outdoor temperature along a heating curve, and a PID on the room temperature can trim that setpoint.

> [!NOTE]
> Weather compensation (equitherm regulation) is how most European boiler controllers work. Instead of reacting to
> the room temperature alone, the controller raises the flow temperature as it gets colder outside, which keeps the
> room temperature steadier and the boiler running at lower, more efficient temperatures.

## How It Works

- **Cold outside** means the boiler gets hotter water.
- **Mild outside** means the boiler gets cooler water.
- **You** set the target room temperature; the curve does the rest.

### The Heating Curve

$$T_{flow} = T_{target} + shift + hc \cdot (T_{target} - T_{outdoor})^{1/n}$$

| Variable | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| T_flow | Flow temperature sent to the boiler, kept between `min_flow_temp` and `max_flow_temp` |
| T_target | The target room temperature |
| T_outdoor | The outdoor temperature from `outdoor_sensor` |
| hc | `heat_curve_coefficient`: how strongly the flow temperature follows the cold |
| n | `heat_curve_exponent`: how the radiators give off heat |
| shift | `heat_curve_shift`: moves the whole curve up or down |

With `hc = 2.0`, `n = 1.25` and a target of 21 °C:

| Outdoor | Flow |
|---------|------|
| -10 °C | 52 °C |
| 0 °C | 44 °C |
| 10 °C | 35 °C |
| 20 °C | 25 °C (the minimum) |

Once it is as warm outside as the target, there is no heating demand: the climate reports `IDLE`, nothing is sent
to the boiler and a float output is set to `0`.

### Which Setting To Change

| Setting | Question it answers | When to change it |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| `heat_curve_coefficient` | How hard does the heating react to cold? | Too cold in winter: raise. Too warm: lower. |
| `heat_curve_exponent` | What kind of radiators are there? | Set once: 1.0 underfloor, 1.25 panels, 1.3 cast iron. |
| `heat_curve_shift` | Is the whole curve too high or too low? | Too cold in mild weather: raise. Too warm: lower. |

Typical values of `heat_curve_coefficient` are 1.0 to 1.5 for a well insulated building, 1.5 to 2.0 for average
insulation and 2.0 to 2.5 for an older building.

### PID Correction

The PID adds a correction from the difference between the room temperature and the target, for what the curve
cannot know about: sunshine, people, cooking. It is off until at least one of `kp`, `ki` or `kd` is set, and it is
only stepped while the indoor sensor reports a value.

## Configuration

```yaml
# Example configuration entry
climate:
  - platform: equitherm
    name: "Central Heating"
    outdoor_sensor: outdoor_temperature
    indoor_sensor: room_temperature
    default_target_temperature: 21°C
    flow_setpoint: boiler_setpoint
    control_parameters:
      heat_curve_coefficient: 2.0
      heat_curve_exponent: 1.25
    output_parameters:
      min_flow_temp: 25°C
      max_flow_temp: 70°C
```

## Configuration Variables

- **outdoor_sensor** (**Required**, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The sensor measuring the outdoor
  temperature, the input of the heating curve. While it reports no value or one outside -55 to 50 °C, the last
  setpoint is kept.
- **indoor_sensor** (**Required**, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The sensor measuring the room
  temperature. It is shown as the current temperature and feeds the PID.
- **default_target_temperature** (**Required**, float): The target room temperature in °C used until one is set
  from the frontend or restored.
- **flow_setpoint** (*Optional*, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): A [number](/components/number/) that
  receives the flow temperature in °C, such as the OpenTherm `t_set` number. Exactly one of `flow_setpoint` and
  `heat_output` is required.
- **heat_output** (*Optional*, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): A [float output](/components/output/) that
  receives the flow temperature as a share of the range: `min_flow_temp` is `0.0` and `max_flow_temp` is `1.0`.
  `0.0` is also written when heating stops, so give the boiler its enable signal from `on_heating_start` and
  `on_heating_stop` rather than from the level.
- **manual_flow_temp** (*Optional*, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): A [number](/components/number/) with a
  flow temperature of your own. When set, the climate gets a `Manual` preset that sends this number's value instead
  of the curve and the PID.
- **control_parameters** (**Required**): The heating curve and PID settings, see below.
- **output_parameters** (**Required**): The flow temperature limits, see below.
- **deadband_parameters** (*Optional*): Calms the PID near the target, see below.
- **on_heating_start** (*Optional*, [Automation](/automations/)): Runs when the climate action becomes `HEATING`.
- **on_heating_stop** (*Optional*, [Automation](/automations/)): Runs when the climate action leaves `HEATING`.
- All other options from [Climate](/components/climate/#config-climate).

### Control Parameters

- **heat_curve_coefficient** (**Required**, float): The coefficient `hc`, 0.5 to 5.0.
- **heat_curve_exponent** (**Required**, float): The radiator exponent `n`, 0.5 to 3.0.
- **heat_curve_shift** (*Optional*, float): A flat offset of the curve in °C. Defaults to `0°C`.
- **kp** (*Optional*, float): The proportional gain of the PID. Defaults to `0`.
- **ki** (*Optional*, float): The integral gain of the PID. Defaults to `0`.
- **kd** (*Optional*, float): The derivative gain of the PID. Defaults to `0`.
- **min_integral** (*Optional*, float): The lowest value the integral term may reach, in °C. Defaults to `-1`.
- **max_integral** (*Optional*, float): The highest value the integral term may reach, in °C. Defaults to `1`.
- **derivative_averaging_samples** (*Optional*, int): How many samples the derivative is averaged over, 1 to 16.
  Defaults to `1`, which is no averaging.

### Output Parameters

- **min_flow_temp** (**Required**, float): The lowest flow temperature sent to the boiler, in °C.
- **max_flow_temp** (**Required**, float): The highest flow temperature sent to the boiler, in °C.
- **write_deadband** (*Optional*, float): The smallest change of the setpoint, in °C, that is written to the
  output. The setpoint is sent in steps of 0.1 °C. Defaults to `0.05°C`.

### Deadband Parameters

Inside the deadband around the target the PID gains are scaled down, so the setpoint stops moving for every small
change of the room temperature. This works as on the [PID Climate](/components/climate/pid/#deadband-setup).

- **threshold_high** (**Required**, float): The upper edge of the deadband, in °C above the target.
- **threshold_low** (**Required**, float): The lower edge of the deadband, in °C relative to the target, so usually
  negative.
- **kp_multiplier** (*Optional*, float): The factor on `kp` inside the deadband. Defaults to `0`.
- **ki_multiplier** (*Optional*, float): The factor on `ki` inside the deadband. Defaults to `0`.
- **kd_multiplier** (*Optional*, float): The factor on `kd` inside the deadband. Defaults to `0`.

```yaml
deadband_parameters:
  threshold_high: 0.5°C
  threshold_low: -0.5°C
  ki_multiplier: 0.05
```

## Manual Flow Temperature

```yaml
number:
  - platform: template
    id: manual_flow_temp
    name: "Manual Flow Temperature"
    min_value: 25
    max_value: 70
    step: 1
    optimistic: true
    restore_value: true
    initial_value: 40

climate:
  - platform: equitherm
    # ...
    manual_flow_temp: manual_flow_temp
```

Selecting the `Manual` preset sends the number's value to the boiler; selecting `None` returns to the curve.

## OpenTherm Example

With the [OpenTherm](/components/opentherm/) component the flow temperature goes to the boiler's `t_set` number,
and the heating triggers switch central heating on and off.

```yaml
switch:
  - platform: opentherm
    ch_enable:
      id: ch_enable

number:
  - platform: opentherm
    t_set:
      id: ch_setpoint
      min_value: 25
      max_value: 70

climate:
  - platform: equitherm
    name: "Central Heating"
    outdoor_sensor: outdoor_temperature
    indoor_sensor: room_temperature
    default_target_temperature: 21°C
    flow_setpoint: ch_setpoint
    on_heating_start:
      - switch.turn_on: ch_enable
    on_heating_stop:
      - switch.turn_off: ch_enable
    control_parameters:
      heat_curve_coefficient: 2.0
      heat_curve_exponent: 1.25
      kp: 0.5
    output_parameters:
      min_flow_temp: 25°C
      max_flow_temp: 70°C
```

## `climate.equitherm.force_recalculate` Action

Recalculates the flow temperature right away, for example after a parameter was changed from a lambda.

```yaml
on_...:
  - climate.equitherm.force_recalculate: heating

on_...:
  - climate.equitherm.force_recalculate:
      id: heating
      update_pid: false
```

- **id** (**Required**, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The equitherm climate.
- **update_pid** (*Optional*, boolean, [templatable](/automations/templates/)): Whether the PID takes a step too.
  With `false` the last correction is kept. Defaults to `true`.

## Tuning

1. Leave the PID gains at `0` and set `heat_curve_exponent` for the radiators.
2. Start with `heat_curve_coefficient: 1.0` and watch the room for a day or two: raise it when the room is too cold
   in cold weather, lower it when too warm. Use `heat_curve_shift` for mild weather in the same way.
3. Add `kp` (0.5 to 1.5) only if the curve alone does not hold the temperature, and a small `ki` (0.001 to 0.01) for
   a lasting offset. `kd` is rarely needed for heating.
