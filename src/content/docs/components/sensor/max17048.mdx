The `max17048` platform allows you to monitor the remaining capacity of a lithium-ion battery with an Analog
Devices MAX17048 or MAX17049 fuel gauge
([datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/MAX17048-MAX17049.pdf)) in
ESPHome. The ModelGauge algorithm in the chip tracks the state of charge over a wide range of charge and
discharge profiles without a sense resistor or relearn cycles. The MAX17048 monitors one cell and the MAX17049
two cells in series; the two chips are otherwise the same. It uses the [I²C Bus](/components/i2c/) for
communication.

> [!NOTE]
> The voltage is scaled for the MAX17048. On a MAX17049, whose voltage step is doubled for its two cells,
> `battery_voltage` is therefore the average voltage per cell rather than the pack voltage; a `multiply: 2` filter
> gives the pack voltage.

```yaml
# Example configuration entry
sensor:
  - platform: max17048
    id: max17048_id
    battery_voltage:
      name: Battery Voltage
    battery_level:
      name: Battery Level
```

## Configuration variables

- **battery_voltage** (*Optional*): The cell voltage in volts.
  All options from [Sensor](/components/sensor/).
- **battery_level** (*Optional*): The state of charge in percent.
  All options from [Sensor](/components/sensor/).
- **rate** (*Optional*): The rate at which the cell is charging or discharging, in percent per hour, positive
  while charging. All options from [Sensor](/components/sensor/).
- **address** (*Optional*, int): The I²C address of the device. Defaults to `0x36`.
- **update_interval** (*Optional*, [Time](/guides/configuration-types/#time)): How often to read the gauge.
  Defaults to `60s`.

If the gauge does not answer when the device starts, for example on an Adafruit Feather that switches the I²C
power on after boot, the component sets a warning and tries again on every update until the gauge answers. The
gauge's CONFIG register keeps its value while the battery is connected; a value left by other firmware is logged
as a warning and written back to the power-up default.

## `max17048.sleep_mode` Action

This action puts the gauge into sleep mode, where it draws less than 1 µA. Call it just before the device enters
deep sleep. When the device starts again, the component wakes the gauge.

```yaml
on_...:
  then:
    - max17048.sleep_mode: max17048_id
    - deep_sleep.enter:
        id: deep_sleep_1
        sleep_duration: 20min
```

Configuration variables:

- **id** (**Required**, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The ID of the MAX17048 sensor.

> [!NOTE]
> While the gauge sleeps it stops measuring, so the sensors keep publishing the *last* values. Only a restart of the
> device wakes it, so only call `sleep_mode` when the device is about to enter deep sleep.
>
> The gauge also cannot track self-discharge while it sleeps. The datasheet recommends its automatic hibernate mode,
> which draws about 3 µA and needs no action, for devices that can afford that current.
>
> Waking from sleep can raise a false low-battery alert in the gauge; the component clears it shortly after waking.
