The TSL235R is a light sensor that outputs a 50% duty-cycle square wave whose frequency is directly proportional to
the irradiance. The output ranges from about 1 Hz in the dark (typically 0.4 Hz) up to roughly 500 kHz.

The part is discontinued by ams-OSRAM, but it is still widely sold on breakout boards. See the
[product page](https://ams-osram.com/products/photodetectors/photodiodes/ams-tsl235-light-to-frequency) for the
datasheet.

## Wiring

The sensor has three pins: `GND`, `VDD` and `OUT`. The supply voltage can be 2.7 V to 5.5 V. Power it from 3.3 V so
that `OUT` is safe to connect directly to an ESP GPIO pin. Connect `OUT` to any GPIO pin that supports input.

## Configuration

Count the output with the [Pulse Counter](/components/sensor/pulse_counter/) sensor:

```yaml
sensor:
  - platform: pulse_counter
    pin: GPIOXX
    name: "Irradiance"
    internal_filter: 0us
    unit_of_measurement: "µW/cm²"
    filters:
      - multiply: 0.0000277778
```

The Pulse Counter reports pulses per minute. The typical responsivity of the TSL235R is 0.6 kHz per µW/cm² at 635 nm,
so the irradiance is the frequency in Hz divided by 600, and the frequency is the pulses per minute divided by 60:

$$
E_e = \frac{f}{600} = \frac{\text{ppm}}{60 \times 600} = \text{ppm} \times 0.0000277778
$$

The `internal_filter` option defaults to `13us`, which on the ESP32 drops pulses shorter than 13 µs. Above roughly
38 kHz (about 64 µW/cm²) pulses would be lost, so the filter is disabled by setting it to `0us`.

## Limitations

- The responsivity depends on the wavelength of the light, and the full-scale tolerance is ±20% between units. The
  result is therefore approximate. For better accuracy, compare the readings with a reference meter and apply a
  [Calibrate Linear](/components/sensor/filter/calibrate_linear/) filter.
- An ESP32 is recommended, as it counts pulses in hardware (PCNT) and handles counter overflow. On the ESP8266, or with
  `use_pcnt: false`, counting is interrupt-based in software and cannot keep up with the high frequencies produced in
  bright light.
