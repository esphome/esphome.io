**Do not generate a title or main heading of your own.** The post already has one –
the developer's own title (e.g. "ESPHome 2026.9.0: Faster builds and encrypted
updates") – and that stays exactly as it is, unchanged, as the only top-level heading on
the page. The companion is not a second post with its own headline; it's a section
that begins directly beneath the existing title. The first thing you generate should

version" (part b) is a subheading within the companion, not a title for it.

**Do not add or infer anything not stated in the sections above.** Every claim in
the companion must trace back to something the finished sections actually say. If

what it means or how much it matters.

### Structure (strict – do not exceed it)

This must be short enough to read in under 30 seconds.

OUTPUT FORMAT: this response is MDX/markdown that will be spliced into the post

### Tone

Write like a friendly, knowledgeable person filling someone in, not a compliance
notice. Vary sentence structure. Lead with the interesting or human part of the
change before the condition. Keep it warm and light – a few words of personality
are fine, but don't force jokes or overdo it.

### Word choice

Never use "flash," "flashing," or "firmware" when writing for beginners. Use
"install" or "update" instead. Rephrase naturally rather than doing a literal
find-and-replace. Define any necessary jargon inline (ESP-IDF, GPIO, coexistence,
watchdog, etc.) in the same sentence, briefly.

### Punctuation

Use en dashes (–), not em dashes (—), for parenthetical asides or connecting
related clauses – e.g. "installing updates gets faster – especially if you manage
more than one device" rather than using an em dash there. This matches house style
used elsewhere on the site.

### Do not include

PR numbers, contributor handles, or specific benchmark figures anywhere in the
companion – this is a companion, not a replacement, and that level of detail
already lives in the full post below.
