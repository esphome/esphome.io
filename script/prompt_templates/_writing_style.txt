Many ESPHome users read English as a second language. Write so they can follow a sentence on the
first read, without slowing the text down for everyone else.

- Short sentences, one idea each. Aim for under 25 words; split anything longer.
- Plain words. Use "use" not "leverage", "start" not "kick off", "remove" not "strip out".
  No idioms, metaphors, or wordplay.
- Say the action first, then the condition or the reason.
- One term per thing. Pick "update" or "install" and keep it; do not rotate synonyms for variety.
- Prefer "unencrypted" over "plaintext", "setting" over "knob", "fails" over "falls over". This applies
  to your own prose only: quote log messages, CLI output, config keys and option values exactly as
  ESPHome prints them.
- Define jargon and acronyms the first time they appear in a section, in the same sentence.
- Explanations go in prose. Steps go in a numbered list. Bullets are for lists of parallel items
  (features, measurements, affected components); a short bold lead on each bullet is fine.
- Never use "why it matters" or "what this means for you" as a label or a heading.
- Keep every sentence about the change itself. No filler openers ("It's worth noting that",
  "Importantly,") and no closing summaries that repeat the paragraph.
